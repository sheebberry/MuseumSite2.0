Dr. Isla Raman

Senior Archaeologist

Background

Dr. Isla Raman is a distinguished archaeologist whose groundbreaking research has fundamentally altered our understanding of ancient ritual practices in high-altitude environments. Born in New Delhi and educated at Oxford University, she completed her doctoral dissertation on "Sacred Landscapes: Ritual Architecture in the Pre-Columbian Andes."

Research Focus

Dr. Raman's primary research interests lie at the intersection of landscape archaeology, religious studies, and material culture. Her work examines how ancient peoples created and maintained sacred spaces in some of the world's most challenging environments—the Himalayas and the Andes mountains.

Over her 15-year career, she has led excavations at over 30 high-altitude sites, uncovering ritual tools, ceremonial structures, and offering caches that provide unprecedented insights into pre-modern spiritual practices. Her innovative use of GIS mapping combined with traditional archaeological methods has revealed complex networks of sacred sites previously unknown to modern scholarship.

Major Achievements

  • Recipient of three Fulbright Fellowships for research in Peru, Nepal, and Tibet
  • Discovery of the Huayna Picchu ritual complex in 2018
  • Author of "Mountains of the Gods: Sacred Architecture in Extreme Environments" (2020)
  • Lead investigator on the International High-Altitude Archaeology Project
  • Winner of the Archaeological Institute of America's Excellence in Field Research Award (2021)

Current Projects

Dr. Raman currently directs the museum's High Altitude Sacred Landscapes Initiative, which combines fieldwork, community engagement, and digital preservation. She is also collaborating with indigenous communities in the Andes to document oral histories related to sacred mountain sites, ensuring that local knowledge is integrated into archaeological interpretation.

Publications

Dr. Raman has published over 60 peer-reviewed articles and contributed to numerous edited volumes on ritual archaeology, landscape studies, and cultural heritage preservation. Her work has been featured in National Geographic, Archaeology Magazine, and Scientific American.

Teaching and Mentorship

In addition to her research, Dr. Raman is passionate about training the next generation of archaeologists. She regularly supervises graduate students and leads field schools in Peru and Nepal, where students gain hands-on experience in excavation techniques and cultural heritage management.
