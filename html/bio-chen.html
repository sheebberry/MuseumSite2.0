Sarah Chen

Lead Cultural Interpreter

Background

Sarah Chen is the Museum of Cultural Memory's Lead Cultural Interpreter and Education Coordinator, where she bridges the gap between scholarly research and public understanding. With a Master's degree in Museum Studies from the George Washington University and a decade of experience in museum education, Sarah is passionate about making complex cultural concepts accessible and engaging for audiences of all ages and backgrounds.

Growing up in San Francisco's diverse cultural landscape, Sarah developed an early appreciation for how museums can serve as spaces of cultural exchange and community dialogue. This background informs her approach to interpretation, which emphasizes inclusivity, accessibility, and multiple perspectives.

Role at the Museum

As Lead Cultural Interpreter, Sarah oversees all public-facing educational programming at the Museum of Cultural Memory. She designs and delivers interactive tours, workshops, and special programs that bring our collections to life. Her innovative approach combines traditional museum interpretation with contemporary engagement techniques, including digital storytelling, hands-on activities, and collaborative learning experiences.

Sarah believes that museums should be conversation spaces rather than lecture halls. Her programs encourage visitors to ask questions, share their own cultural knowledge, and connect museum content to their lived experiences.

Program Development

Sarah has developed numerous award-winning programs during her tenure at the museum, including:

  • Cultural Connections: A monthly series bringing together community members to share stories and traditions related to museum exhibitions
  • Junior Archaeologists: A summer program where children learn archaeological methods through hands-on simulations
  • Immigrant Voices: An oral history project documenting and exhibiting the cultural heritage of recent immigrant communities
  • Museum After Dark: Evening programs for adults featuring curator talks, cultural performances, and themed experiences
  • Memory Workshops: Interactive sessions exploring how different cultures preserve and transmit cultural memory

Community Outreach

Beyond the museum walls, Sarah coordinates extensive community outreach initiatives. She has established partnerships with local schools, community centers, senior facilities, and cultural organizations to ensure that the museum serves as a resource for the entire community.

Her "Museum on Wheels" program brings artifacts, activities, and programming directly to communities that face barriers to visiting the physical museum, including rural schools, hospitals, and correctional facilities.

Volunteer Coordination

Sarah manages the museum's robust volunteer program, which includes over 50 active volunteers who serve as tour guides, program assistants, and community liaisons. She developed a comprehensive training curriculum that equips volunteers not only with content knowledge but also with skills in cultural sensitivity, inclusive communication, and visitor engagement.

Under her leadership, the volunteer program has become a model for other institutions, winning recognition from the American Alliance of Museums.

Professional Development and Recognition

  • Master of Arts in Museum Studies, George Washington University (2014)
  • Bachelor of Arts in Cultural Anthropology, University of California, Berkeley (2011)
  • Certified Museum Educator through the Museum Education Roundtable
  • Recipient of the Regional Museums Association's Excellence in Education Award (2022)
  • Regular presenter at museum education conferences nationwide

Philosophy of Museum Education

Sarah believes that effective museum interpretation requires balancing scholarship with accessibility, respecting both the expertise of researchers and the lived experiences of visitors. She advocates for interpretive approaches that acknowledge multiple perspectives, invite dialogue, and empower visitors to become active participants in cultural preservation.

"Museums aren't just repositories of the past," Sarah often says. "They're living spaces where we collectively decide which stories to remember, which voices to amplify, and how to carry cultural memory into the future."

Current Projects

Sarah is currently developing a digital interpretation platform that will allow visitors to continue their learning journey beyond their museum visit. This platform will feature curator interviews, artifact deep-dives, community-contributed stories, and interactive activities. She is also working on a bilingual interpretation initiative to make all museum content accessible in Spanish and Mandarin.
