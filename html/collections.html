Name: Clovis Projectile Point

Material: Cryptocrystalline flint (chert)

Size/Weight/Shape: Length 10.4 cm; width 3.1 cm; thickness 0.7 cm; lanceolate, slightly convex edges; very light

Estimated Age: ~13,200–12,800 BP (Late Pleistocene); inferred from stratigraphic layer and radiocarbon dated megafauna remains in same horizon

Preservation State: Nearly complete; minor tip rounding and micro-fractures along cutting edge

Location Found: Blackwater Draw Locality No. 1, New Mexico, USA; recovered from buried paleosol layer associated with extinct bison

Likely Purpose: Hafted spear point used in thrusting or atlatl hunting of large game

Evidence of Use: Impact fracture at distal end, edge micro-chipping, polish near hafting base from binding material

Manufacture Clues: Advanced pressure flaking; fluting suggests intentional thinning for haft insertion and shock absorption

Found With: Mammoth and bison bone fragments, scrapers, blade flakes, charcoal lenses

Burial/Habitat Context: Open-air kill and butchering site, not habitation

Symbolism: No decorative features; utilitarian survival technology

Comparison: Classic Clovis morphology consistent with widespread North American Paleoindian toolkit
